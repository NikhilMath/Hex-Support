# Hex Support: Till Death — production notes

A 12-minute short built from the shortest route to the Till Death ending:
**The Tower → Off and On Again → Mama? → Law of the Dead → What Doris Wants →
Team Plonk → Doris's Grave → Till Death.** Almost all of the dialogue comes
straight from the book.

The script is in `till-death.fountain`, a plain-text screenplay format. It
opens formatted in Highland, Beat, Fade In, WriterSolo, or the free
[fountain.io](https://fountain.io) tools.

## What it takes

| | |
|---|---|
| Actors on camera | 3: the Tech, Plonk, Doris |
| Voices only | Orb, Marrow, Deb from dispatch (one person can do all three) |
| Cameos | Gary (one shot, one line, no shirt needed... no shirt *wanted*) |
| Locations | 2 real ones: a room for the tower, a grassy spot for the cemetery. Plus any stairwell and any mailbox. |
| Shoot | A weekend. Day 1: tower (it's one long scene). Day 2: stairwell, mailbox, then cemetery at sunset. |

## Cheap tricks

- **The tower.** Any room. Hang a grey bedsheet or stone-print fabric
  behind the desk, light it with candles plus one lamp, and clutter the desk
  with books and junk. Shoot tight so the walls barely show.
- **The orb.** A frosted glass lamp globe or a fishbowl with a colour LED
  bulb or a phone torch inside. Blue and flickering before the reboot, soft
  pink after. The orb's voice is added in the edit, so the actors just talk to
  a lamp.
- **The receipt summons.** Feed a strip of receipt paper up through a slot in
  the cushion from underneath, off camera. The pop-up "update" box near the
  end is a sticker or a cheap screen overlay in the edit.
- **Wizcast.** Shoot Doris and Marrow first in a closet with a black sheet,
  then play that footage on the laptop while you shoot the tower scene. The
  actors get something real to react to and you don't need any compositing.
- **Doris.** Half-decayed is half a face of latex and tissue (or liquid latex
  and cereal), grey makeup and eyeliner, with the other half done up nicely:
  pearls, good hair. Shoot her nose-to-lens line with a phone held right
  against her face.
- **Marrow.** A store-bought Halloween skeleton (they're half price on
  November 1) in a clip-on tie, with someone off camera moving its head on
  the lines. A sand timer or egg timer for the hourglass.
- **The Silverlight cape.** A thrift-shop cape with a battery LED fairy-light
  strip sewn inside the lining, plus as many little padlocks as you can find.
  The hum is a sound effect.
- **Doris rising from the grave.** The headstone is painted insulation foam
  (hardware store, a few dollars). She crouches behind it and stands up
  slowly. A fog machine or dry ice adds a lot, and a light hidden in the grass
  gives the glow. Shoot at golden hour and the sun does the rest.
- **Plonk turning to dust.** Every bit of it is jump cuts, so nothing has to
  move. Lock the camera off. Shoot him with his normal beard, then put baby
  powder in it, then put on a grey beard, then cut to his empty robe and hat on
  a heap of flour. The sneeze is a puff of air through a straw under the robe.
- **Ghost Plonk.** Same actor, pale makeup, a bit of flour in the eyebrows,
  and a blue colour grade in the edit for any shot with a ghost in it.
- **The pigeon.** A craft-store fake pigeon on the headstone, with a coo
  sound effect. If a real one shows up, roll on it.
- **The work crystal.** A chunk of glass or a resin "crystal" with a small
  LED inside, on a Hex Support lanyard. The buzz is a phone on vibrate in
  your hand, just off screen.

## Rough budget

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Plonk's robe, hat and beard, the cape, padlocks | $60 |
| Doris's makeup and latex | $30 |
| Halloween skeleton, egg timer | $25 |
| Orb globe and colour LED bulb | $20 |
| Foam headstone, paint | $15 |
| Fog machine (rent or borrow) | $0–40 |
| Fake pigeon, flour, receipt roll, lanyard | $15 |
| Food for a weekend crew | $100 |
| **Total** | **about $265–305** |

Shoot it on a phone. Spend the money you save on a decent lav mic: people
forgive cheap pictures, but not bad sound.
